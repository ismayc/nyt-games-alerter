Puzzles that cheat a different way

Some puzzles hide the trick inside a single square that quietly accepts a second answer, a Schrodinger clue, a symbol, or an April Fools swap, rather than cramming several letters in. Those are a different device from a rebus, so they are tracked on their own: 92 of the 11,991 daily puzzles scanned have at least one.

Method and limits

  • Source: the puzzle JSON that nytimes.com serves to its own game pages, read without a login. A rebus is any square whose stored answer is longer than one character.
  • Cross-checked: a sample of rebus dates was confirmed against XWord Info's independent rebus list, and every stored value re-derived from the live puzzle on the last full scan.
  • A gimmick square is a single letter cell that still accepts another answer. Ordinary puzzles have none; visual-only gimmicks leave no trace and are not counted.
  • Coverage: 11,991 of the 11,991 calendar days from November 21, 1993 to September 19, 2026 have a daily crossword on record.
  • Refreshed on the first of each month. Last built September 19, 2026; next refresh October 1, 2026.

Beyond the pattern

What makes a Wordle nasty

__A_E is one shape. These are the traits Wordle players grumble about, counted across the 1,918 answers on record.

  • Repeated a letter: 597 answers (31.1%). 13 used one letter three times, like SISSY, ERROR, FLUFF, MUMMY, NANNY, DADDY.
  • Leaned on a rare letter (J, Q, X, or Z): 112 answers, like QUIET, MAJOR, HELIX, CRAZY, MAXIM, FJORD.
  • The cruelest near-miss: LIGHT is a single letter away from 8 other answers in the original list, the classic one-blank coin flip. 133 answers sat in a look-alike family of five or more.

Method and limits

  • Source: the Wordle JSON that nytimes.com serves to the game, read without a login. Every match is derived from the stored answer, so adding a pattern needs no re-fetch.
  • The gripe counts (repeats, rare letters, look-alike families) are computed from the answers on record; the family sizes use the original Wordle answer list.
  • Cross-checked: stored answers were spot-checked against public Wordle archives, and 97% appear in the original answer word list (the rest are real words the NYT added later).
  • The patterns live in one place, WORDLE_PATTERNS, currently __A_E and _A_E_.
  • Refreshed on the first of each month. Last built September 19, 2026; next refresh October 1, 2026.
